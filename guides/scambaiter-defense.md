## What the developers say

Steam warns players about scambaiters and computer viruses. The developer website also mentions scambaiters in the remote-access feature description. This establishes a counter-hacking risk in the fictional game.

## What is not established

The cited sources do not identify named scambaiter characters, a guaranteed tell in a voice, a fixed encounter probability, a reverse-lock timer, or a particular recovery item. They do not establish that any real-world creator appears as a game character.

## Suggested way to investigate a suspicious call

Use the current game's visible prompts and available controls. If the call or desktop behaves unexpectedly, record what happened and which game build you used. Do not treat a generic caller trait or a single odd reply as proof of a special encounter.

If you are making a community report, separate the observation from your interpretation: describe the actual response, then explain why you think it may be a scambaiter. Include the recovery steps that actually worked in that build.

## Rehearsal versus game evidence

The site's [dialogue exercise](https://scamwithyourfriendsguide.online/tools/call-simulator/) uses text written for this website. Its choices and responses cannot validate the game's AI, detection logic, rewards, or penalties.
